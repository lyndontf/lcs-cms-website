<!-- ─────────────────── UNDERSTANDING ─────────────────── -->
<section class="sr-sec" id="understand">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="sr-sec-head">
      <span class="eyebrow"><span class="dot"></span> The basics</span>
      <h2>What is a stroke?</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="sr-split">
      <div class="sr-prose">
      <p><strong>A stroke is a "brain attack."</strong> It happens when the blood supply to part of the brain is suddenly cut off, so brain cells are starved of oxygen and begin to die. Depending on which part of the brain is affected, a stroke can change movement, speech, vision, swallowing, memory and emotions.</p>
      <p>There are two main types. An <strong>ischaemic stroke</strong> (about 85% of cases) is caused by a clot blocking a blood vessel. A <strong>haemorrhagic stroke</strong> happens when a blood vessel bursts and bleeds into the brain. A <strong>TIA</strong> ("mini-stroke") has the same signs but clears within minutes or hours — it must never be ignored, as it's a serious warning of a possible bigger stroke.</p>
      <div class="sr-callout">
        <p><b>Why time matters so much.</b> With every minute that treatment is delayed, more brain cells are lost. Getting to hospital fast can mean the difference between a small setback and a lasting disability — which is why the F.A.S.T. signs above are so important.</p>
      </div>
      <p>The most hopeful thing to know: <strong>the brain can re-learn.</strong> Through a process called neuroplasticity, healthy parts of the brain can take over lost functions. With early, consistent rehabilitation, most survivors make meaningful progress — and many return to the activities and independence they love.</p>
      </div>
      <img class="sr-realimg" src="/images/strokerehab/understand.jpg" alt="A caregiver warmly helping an elderly stroke survivor at home">
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

      <span class="eyebrow"><span class="dot"></span> Common questions</span>
      <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="sr-faq">
      <div class="sr-q"><button onclick="srFaq(this)"><span>Can you fully recover from a stroke?</span><span class="pm"><svg width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2.5"><path stroke-linecap="round" d="M12 5v14M5 12h14"/></svg></span></button><div class="ans"><p>Many people do make a full or near-full recovery, especially with early, consistent rehabilitation — though the extent depends on the size and location of the stroke.       <div class="sr-q"><button onclick="srFaq(this)"><span>When should rehabilitation start?</span><span class="pm"><svg width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2.5"><path stroke-linecap="round" d="M12 5v14M5 12h14"/></svg></span></button><div class="ans"><p>As early as it's safe — often within days, in hospital.
