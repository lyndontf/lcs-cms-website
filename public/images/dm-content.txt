<!-- ─────────────────── UNDERSTANDING ─────────────────── -->
<section class="dm-sec" id="understand">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="dm-sec-head">
      <span class="eyebrow"><span class="dot"></span> The basics</span>
      <h2>What is dementia?</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="dm-split">
      <div class="dm-prose">
      <p><strong>Dementia is not a single disease.</strong> It's an umbrella term for a group of symptoms — memory loss, confusion, difficulty thinking, communicating and managing daily life — caused by different diseases that damage the brain. Alzheimer's disease is the most common cause, but there are several others.</p>
      <p>The most important thing to know: <strong>dementia is not a normal part of getting older.</strong> Many older people stay sharp into their 90s. While occasionally forgetting a name and remembering it later is normal ageing, dementia involves changes serious enough to interfere with everyday life — and they gradually get worse over time.</p>
      <div class="dm-callout">
        <p><b>Dementia vs. Alzheimer's — what's the difference?</b><br>Think of <b>dementia</b> as the set of symptoms, and <b>Alzheimer's disease</b> as the most common cause of those symptoms (about 60–80% of cases). Someone can have dementia caused by Alzheimer's, by strokes (vascular dementia), or by several causes at once.</p>
      </div>
      <p>There is currently no cure, but <strong>dementia can be managed</strong>. The right support, a calm and structured environment, meaningful activity and good care can slow decline, reduce distress, and help your loved one live well for as long as possible. Early understanding makes everything that follows easier.</p>
      </div>

    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="dm-sec alt" id="signs">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="dm-sec-head">
      <span class="eyebrow"><span class="dot"></span> Spotting it early</span>
      <h2>10 early warning signs</h2>
      <p>One sign on its own is rarely cause for alarm. But if you notice several of these, and they're getting worse, it's worth speaking to a doctor.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dm-grid3">
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">1</div><h3>Memory loss that disrupts life</h3><p>Forgetting recently learned information, important dates, or asking the same questions over and over.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">2</div><h3>Trouble with familiar tasks</h3><p>Struggling to cook a long-known recipe, manage finances, or follow a familiar route.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">3</div><h3>Confusion with time or place</h3><p>Losing track of dates and seasons, or forgetting where they are and how they got there.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">4</div><h3>Problems with words</h3><p>Difficulty following conversations, stopping mid-sentence, or calling things by the wrong name.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">5</div><h3>Misplacing things</h3><p>Putting items in unusual places and being unable to retrace steps — sometimes accusing others of stealing.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">6</div><h3>Poor judgement</h3><p>Falling for scams, neglecting hygiene, or making uncharacteristic decisions with money.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">7</div><h3>Withdrawing socially</h3><p>Pulling away from hobbies, work projects, family gatherings or religious activities they once enjoyed.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">8</div><h3>Mood & personality changes</h3><p>Becoming confused, suspicious, fearful, anxious or easily upset — especially outside their comfort zone.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">9</div><h3>Trouble with vision & space</h3><p>Difficulty judging distance, reading, or recognising faces — which can affect driving and balance.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-sign"><div class="n">10</div><h3>Difficulty planning</h3><p>Trouble following a plan, working with numbers, or concentrating on multi-step tasks.</p></div>

<!-- ─────────────────── TYPES ─────────────────── -->
<section class="dm-sec" id="types">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="dm-sec-head">
      <span class="eyebrow"><span class="dot"></span> Knowing the cause</span>
      <h2>The main types of dementia</h2>
      <p>Different diseases cause dementia in different ways. Knowing the type helps families and care teams plan the right support.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dm-grid3">
      <div class="dm-type"><span class="tag">Most common</span><h3>Alzheimer's Disease</h3><p>Accounts for 60–80% of cases. Begins with short-term memory loss and gradually affects language, reasoning and orientation as brain cells are progressively damaged.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-type"><span class="tag">2nd most common</span><h3>Vascular Dementia</h3><p>Caused by reduced blood flow to the brain, often after a stroke or series of mini-strokes. May appear suddenly and progress in "steps" rather than smoothly.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-type"><span class="tag">Fluctuating</span><h3>Lewy Body Dementia</h3><p>Marked by fluctuating alertness, visual hallucinations, sleep disturbance and movement problems similar to Parkinson's. Needs patient, adaptive care.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-type"><span class="tag">Behaviour & language</span><h3>Frontotemporal Dementia</h3><p>Affects the front of the brain — changing personality, behaviour and language, sometimes before memory. Often appears at a younger age (50s–60s).</p></div>
      <div class="dm-type"><span class="tag">Movement & memory</span><h3>Parkinson's Dementia</h3><p>Some people with Parkinson's develop dementia as the condition advances, combining movement difficulties with changes in memory and thinking.</p></div>
      <div class="dm-type"><span class="tag">Overlapping</span><h3>Mixed Dementia</h3><p>When more than one type occurs together — most often Alzheimer's combined with vascular dementia. Requires a holistic, coordinated care plan.</p></div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="dm-sec lav" id="stages">

      <h2>The stages of dementia</h2>
      <p>Dementia is progressive, but everyone's journey is different. These stages are a general guide — not a fixed timetable.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dm-stages">
      <div class="dm-stage s1">
        <div class="top"><h3>Early stage</h3><span>Mild</span></div>
        <div class="body"><ul>
          <li>Forgetfulness and repeating questions</li>
          <li>Losing track of time or familiar routes</li>
          <li>Trouble finding the right words</li>
          <li>Still largely independent with daily tasks</li>
          <li><b>Focus:</b> diagnosis, planning ahead, staying active and social</li>
        </ul></div>
      </div>
      <div class="dm-stage s2">
        <div class="top"><h3>Middle stage</h3><span>Moderate</span></div>
        <div class="body"><ul>
          <li>Needs help with dressing, bathing and meals</li>
          <li>Increasing confusion, may not recognise people</li>
          <li>Restlessness, wandering or sundowning</li>
          <li>Changes in mood and behaviour</li>
          <li><b>Focus:</b> safety, structure, routine and supervision</li>
        </ul></div>
      </div>
      <div class="dm-stage s3">
        <div class="top"><h3>Late stage</h3><span>Advanced</span></div>
        <div class="body"><ul>
          <li>Full-time care with all daily activities</li>
          <li>Limited speech and mobility</li>
          <li>Difficulty swallowing and eating</li>
          <li>Vulnerable to infections</li>
          <li><b>Focus:</b> comfort, dignity, skilled nursing and emotional presence</li>
        </ul></div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="dm-callout" style="margin-top:26px">
      <p><b>The journey changes — so should the care.</b> Many families manage well at home in the early stage, then consider specialist memory care as needs grow. There's no "right" time except the one that keeps your loved one safe and well, and keeps you from burning out.</p>

<section class="dm-sec" id="caregiving">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="dm-sec-head">
      <span class="eyebrow"><span class="dot"></span> Day to day</span>
      <h2>Caring for someone at home</h2>
      <p>Small changes in how you communicate and structure the day can make a big difference — for them and for you.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="dm-grid2">
      <div class="dm-tip">
        <div class="ic"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.9"><path stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" d="M8 10h8M8 14h5M21 12a8 8 0 01-8 8H7l-4 3v-6a8 8 0 1118-5z"/></svg></div>

      </div>
      <div class="dm-tip">
        <div class="ic"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.9"><path stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round" d="M12 6v6l4 2M12 22a10 10 0 100-20 10 10 0 000 20z"/></svg></div>
        <div><h3>Keep a steady routine</h3><p>Regular times for meals, bathing, activity and sleep reduce confusion and anxiety. Familiarity is calming — keep belongings and furniture in the same places.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="dm-tip">
        <div class="ic"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.9" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"><path d="M3 11.5l9-7 9 7"/><path d="M5 10v9.5a.5.5 0 00.5.5h13a.5.5 0 00.5-.5V10"/><path d="M9.3 14.6l1.7 1.7 3.4-3.4"/></svg></div>

<section class="dm-sec alt" id="faq">

    <div class="dm-sec-head center">
      <span class="eyebrow"><span class="dot"></span> Common questions</span>
      <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="dm-faq">
      <div class="dm-q">
        <button onclick="dmFaq(this)"><span>What's the difference between dementia and Alzheimer's?</span><span class="pm"><svg width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2.5"><path stroke-linecap="round" d="M12 5v14M5 12h14"/></svg></span></button>
        <div class="ans"><p>Dementia is an umbrella term for symptoms affecting memory, thinking and daily function. Alzheimer's disease is the most common <em>cause</em> of dementia (around 60–80% of cases). Other causes include vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia and frontotemporal dementia.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="dm-q">
        <button onclick="dmFaq(this)"><span>Is dementia a normal part of getting old?</span><span class="pm"><svg width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2.5"><path stroke-linecap="round" d="M12 5v14M5 12h14"/></svg></span></button>
        <div class="ans"><p>No. While some mild forgetfulness can come with age, dementia involves changes serious enough to interfere with everyday life, and they progressively worsen. It's caused by diseases that damage the brain — not by ageing itself. That's why it's worth getting memory changes checked.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="dm-q">
        <button onclick="dmFaq(this)"><span>How do I know if my parent needs memory care?</span><span class="pm"><svg width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2.5"><path stroke-linecap="round" d="M12 5v14M5 12h14"/></svg></span></button>
        <div class="ans"><p>Common signs include safety incidents (wandering, falls, leaving the stove on), difficulty managing medication, round-the-clock supervision needs, and caregiver burnout. If you're unsure, a free assessment with a care advisor can help you understand the level of care needed — <a href="https://genesiscare.com.my/contact">book one here</a>.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="dm-q">
        <button onclick="dmFaq(this)"><span>Can dementia be cured or slowed?</span><span class="pm"><svg width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2.5"><path stroke-linecap="round" d="M12 5v14M5 12h14"/></svg></span></button>
        <div class="ans"><p>There's currently no cure, but dementia can be <em>managed</em>. A calm, structured environment, meaningful activity, good nutrition, and evidence-based programmes like Cognitive Stimulation Therapy (CST) can help maintain function, reduce distress and improve quality of life.</p></div>
      </div>
      <div class="dm-q">
        <button onclick="dmFaq(this)"><span>What financial help is available in Malaysia?</span><span class="pm"><svg width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2.5"><path stroke-linecap="round" d="M12 5v14M5 12h14"/></svg></span></button>
        <div class="ans"><p>Options include the RM8,000 parental-care income-tax relief (which explicitly covers nursing-home costs), EPF Akaun Fleksibel withdrawals, JKM welfare aid, zakat for eligible Muslim families, and schemes like mySalam and PeKa B40. See the <a href="#malaysia">Support in Malaysia</a> section above, and Genesis advisors can help you navigate them.</p></div>

      <div class="dm-q">
        <button onclick="dmFaq(this)"><span>What is Cognitive Stimulation Therapy (CST)?</span><span class="pm"><svg width="14" height="14" viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="2.5"><path stroke-linecap="round" d="M12 5v14M5 12h14"/></svg></span></button>
